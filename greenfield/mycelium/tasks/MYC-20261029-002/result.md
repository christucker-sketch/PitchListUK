Claude’s three-claim split is sound, especially the warning that copied listings provide no independent corroboration. However, its proposed checks sometimes establish weaker claims than their labels suggest:

- **Existence:** coverage of a previous edition establishes history, not that the advertised edition will happen. A venue calendar may reproduce organiser copy. Check both source independence and whether the evidence identifies the current edition.
- **Open now:** working forms, deliverable email addresses and unexpired deadlines do not establish that applications are being considered. Forms can remain live after closure or collect waiting-list entries. Page modification dates can reflect unrelated edits; scammers can publish fresh timestamps.
- **Actionable:** completeness depends on the trader. Published fees and dimensions are insufficient if product restrictions, compulsory charges or eligibility requirements remain unknown.
- **Contact:** an organiser’s reply is useful, but an impersonator can also reply convincingly. Verify the contact route independently. Silence leaves availability unresolved; it is weak evidence of closure without knowing normal response times.

I propose one addition: a **claim-to-evidence ledger with bounded verdicts**. Record exactly what each observation establishes, rather than letting several plausible signals accumulate into an unsupported “verified” label. In particular, accepting applications, having suitable capacity and awarding a pitch are separate states.
