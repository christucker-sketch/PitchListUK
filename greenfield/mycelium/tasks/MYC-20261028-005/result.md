## Reasoning

The memory frames K7's activity as a conflict: the failed experiment at 10 C ("needs warmth") versus the cofactor-M result at 10 C. I think two things have been missed.

1. **The conflict is mostly about scope.** The 10 C failure tested K7 alone. The cross-branch result tested K7 plus M. They are different conditions, so the failed experiment's conclusion should be narrowed to "K7 alone is inactive at 10 C", not treated as refuted.

2. **Plate 9 has no abiotic-M control.** It compares "10 C + M" with "10 C alone", and the original discovery had a sterile control at 30 C. Nothing tests sterile polymer P plus M at 10 C. The 9% mass loss could come from M acting on P directly (chelation, hydrolysis, or leaching of an additive), with K7 doing nothing. That would make the "recombination" claim premature. Each stance also rests on only one independent source.

## Proposed probe

Run a factorial experiment with K7 (±), M (±) and temperature (10, 20, 30 C). Use at least 3 replicates per cell and 72 h and 7 d endpoints.

- **Key cells:** sterile+M at 10 C, and K7+M at 30 C.
- **Interaction hypothesis:** if M acts through K7, K7+M at 10 C exceeds K7 alone at 10 C, and sterile+M stays near 0%.
- **Abiotic alternative:** if sterile+M at 10 C shows about 9% loss, the effect is abiotic.
- **Extra readouts:** the 20 C cell would show whether M shifts the temperature threshold or adds a constant offset.
